sReader Story Trek
The complete 2D reading game design

The full destination, mapped before implementation begins: every phase designed end-to-end — reading gameplay, quest map, multiplayer, scheduling, meta progression — plus the art direction, VFX, audio and juice spec that makes it a fancy game. Reading is the core mechanic throughout; every FR keeps a concrete home; Tutor, Guardian and auth screens stay per spec.

Design principles for an FR-first game

The FR document changes the brief in three important ways, and this version is built around them.

First, sReader is a multi-role product, so the game is a layer, not a rewrite. Tutors do CRUD (academies, grades, courses, assignments, submissions, payments) and guardians do oversight — gamifying those would hurt them. The game applies only to the student play surface: the Home dashboard, the Assignments section, and above all the reading game (solo and multiplayer). Everything else — Landing, About, Contact, the whole auth stack, Profile, Tutor Home, Guardian Home — is already specified and stays exactly as per the FRs, with at most a shared art style pass.

Second, "at least a 2D game" means real 2D scenes, not styled panels. The reading game (FR-SHOME-5.3) and the multiplayer board (FR-SHOME-5.4) move out of pure UI Toolkit into Unity 2D scenes: SpriteRenderers, parallax background layers, a Tilemap-or-spline path, and an animated player character. UI Toolkit remains the overlay layer (HUD, challenge panels, dialogs), which keeps your MVVM pattern and existing challenge logic intact — the FRs' architecture note ("controllers hold no business logic") continues to hold, with the 2D scene as just another view bound to the same ViewModels.

Third, the game must serve the LMS contract. Assignments have due dates, marks out of 20, attempt states, schedules, offline downloads and tutor-reviewed submissions. The game reframes these but never hides them: a due date is a storm rolling toward an island, but the date is still printed on the flag; the trek result is still recorded through the existing submission path (FR-SHOME-5.3) and the tutor sees raw marks, not stars.

The fictionEach assignment is a trail through the Knowledge Wilds. The passage your tutor wrote is the terrain; every hidden word is a gate across the path. Solve the gate's challenge — the exact challenges you've already built — and the trail continues.
The characterKai, your existing reading-boy mascot, promoted from decoration to playable character. He walks the trail, celebrates at gates, and plants a checkpoint flag wherever you stop — which is your per-device resume point.
The multiplayerThe FRs already specify a turn-based board with lobby, presence and live events. That's a board-game race, so we render it as one: tokens, tiles, turn marker, emotes floating over players.
The contractMarks out of 20, due dates, submissions, schedules — all first-class and visible. Stars, XP and streaks are computed from them, never instead of them.

The student loop

The loop matches the assignment lifecycle the FRs already define — nothing is invented, only re-lit:

Reading is the mechanic, not the obstacle

This is a reading game. Every system below is judged by one question: does it make the child read more, read better, and want to reread?

Fluency — the ribbon paces the walk. The story ribbon and Kai are one system: the passage advances as Kai walks, so the reading is the traversal. Words already read stay dimmed behind the reading head; the current sentence is highlighted; upcoming text is visible but muted, pulling the eye forward. An optional read-along mode (settings toggle) highlights word-by-word at an adjustable pace for emerging readers — Kai simply walks at the pace the child reads.

Vocabulary — gates are the passage's own key terms. Gates aren't random puzzles; they are the words the tutor hid in the passage, met in context first (the child reads up to the blank, so the sentence frames the word before the puzzle asks for it). Every conquered word is captured into the Word Journal (screen 5 below) with its sentence, definition and image — vocabulary as a collectible the child owns and revisits.

Comprehension — the trail remembers what you read. The Pass detour never lets a word escape unlearned: after skirting a gate, the trail sign shows the answer ("it was concentration — it'll be in your Journal in grey until you win it"). Skipped words appear in the Journal as silhouettes, a gentle pull to replay. A future gate type — Campfire Questions (short comprehension checks at trail's end) — is designed-for but out of scope until tutors can author them.

Rereading — replays are the star economy. Stars reward mastery (speed + no hints + no detours), and mastery of a passage requires rereading it. "Reset trail" (FR-SHOME-5.2) is therefore not remediation but the path to ★★★ and to turning silhouette words gold in the Journal.

Screen-by-screen (student surface only)

Nine screens cover the entire student game surface. Together with the flow diagram at the end, this is the whole destination.

BeforeYour current dashboard already satisfies FR-SHOME-1 (hero, stats row, chips, quick links, fixed layout, mascot) — functionally it's right; it just reads as an app.

AfterSame information architecture, rendered as Kai's camp at the edge of the Wilds. AFTER — Base Camp. Hero priority, stats row, chips, quick links and the fixed non-scrolling layout are exactly FR-SHOME-1; only the rendering changes. The offline banner strip (FR-SHELL-5) reserves the top edge.

2 · Assignments list → Quest Map

BeforeThe list correctly carries status, class, due date and schedule per FR-SHOME-5 — as cards.

AfterAssignments become islands on a trail through the Wilds, ordered by due date. Every FR-SHOME-5.x capability keeps a visible home: the filter row (5.1) becomes the map legend chips and still filters literally (islands fade out); search and the class filter stay in the top bar; long-press or tap an island opens the same detail sheet with Start / Resume / Reset trail / Submit / Schedule / Download (5.2, 5.5, 5.6). Urgency is diegetic and literal: a storm cloud crawls toward islands as the due date nears, turns the flag red when overdue — and the date is still printed. A downloaded assignment shows a backpack tick (5.6); a scheduled one shows a tent with the time (5.5). The empty state (5.8) is Kai with a spyglass: "No trails yet — discover an academy", deep-linking to Academies → Discover (FR-SHELL-7).

AFTER — Quest Map. Filters (FR-SHOME-5.1) are the legend; schedule tent (5.5), download backpack (5.6), reset/submit (5.2) live on the island's detail sheet; due dates stay printed while the storm dramatises them.

3 · The reading game → The Trek (a real 2D scene)

BeforeYour current reading game already has strong mechanics — passage with hidden words, timer, points, Pass, emotes — presented as a scrolling text panel.

AfterThis is the heart of the redesign, and where "at least 2D" is spent. The Trek is a side-view 2D scene: parallax sky, mountains and treeline; a ground path; Kai walking it. The tutor's passage is the trail itself — the text scrolls along a story ribbon at the bottom, synchronised with Kai's position, and each hidden word in the passage is a physical gate blocking the path ahead. Kai auto-walks (no platforming skill required — this is a reading game, and it must be playable by every student on any device) and stops at each gate; solving it swings the gate open, awards points, and Kai walks on while the next stretch of passage scrolls by. Everything the FRs require is preserved: the timer and points are the HUD; Pass becomes a detour path around the gate (same skip semantics); leaving mid-trek plants a checkpoint flag at the last solved gate — which is exactly the per-device resume point of FR-SHOME-5.3; finishing records the result through the existing submission flow.

AFTER — the Trek. Passage = trail, hidden word = gate, Pass = detour, checkpoint flag = the FR-SHOME-5.3 resume point. Kai auto-walks so reading skill, not reflexes, is what's tested.

Presentation targetThe mockup above is the wireframe; below is the fancy target for the same screen — what Phase 5 polish aims at. Sunset-lit biome, glowing gate rune, firefly particles, a combo flame for consecutive no-hint solves, and the ribbon's active sentence lit like a lantern. Every element is still the same FR-mapped component.

Presentation target — the same Trek, fully dressed: dusk biome, rune-lit gate, fireflies, combo flame, lantern-lit active sentence. Read words dim behind you; the glowing line is where your eyes are.

4 · The word challenges → Gate puzzles (reskin, same logic)

BeforeYou already have three working challenge types: arrange-the-definition, pick-the-image, and spell-the-word — plus Hint (-40%) and Back.

AfterThese survive intact as the three gate types, presented as an in-world panel that slides up when Kai reaches a gate. Same data, same checking code, same Hint (-40%) economy — only framed as the gate's lock. Because the challenge panels stay UI Toolkit overlays, this is mostly USS work, not gameplay code.

AFTER — gate puzzles. Identical mechanics and scoring to today's define / illustrate / fill challenges; the panels remain UI Toolkit overlays bound to the same ViewModels.

5 · New: the Word Journal — vocabulary as collectibles

New in v3The meta-layer that makes this unmistakably a reading game. Every word won at a gate is pressed into Kai's journal as a card: the word, the sentence it lived in, its definition, its picture, and the subject it came from. Perfect solves (no hint, first try) mint a gold-foil card; hint-assisted wins mint silver; words passed by detour appear as grey silhouettes that whisper "come back for me." The Journal is pure derived data — the hidden-word challenges you already store, plus a per-word best-solve record — but it converts vocabulary growth into a collection kids show each other. Journal size feeds the Reader Rank (see roadmap Phase 4), and a "word of the day" resurfaces an old card on Base Camp for spaced repetition.

Screen 5 — the Word Journal. Gold for perfect solves, silver for hint-assisted, grey silhouettes for detoured words that invite a replay. Derived entirely from data you already store.

6 · Play with others → the Campfire (host & join, lobby)

Complete in v3The entry to multiplayer keeps every FR-SHOME-5.4 path — host, join by code, join from the open-sessions list — arranged around one metaphor: light a campfire, friends gather. Hosting pitches a campfire and mints the code (huge, dictation-friendly for a classroom). The backend choice survives as the "fire type" on the host panel — Quick flame (Photon: instant, nothing saved) vs Ember (Supabase: saved games) — technical names printed small, choice remembered per device exactly as specified. The lobby renders presence honestly: friends' avatars take log seats around the fire, the fire flares when someone arrives, a seat greys out on heartbeat loss, and only the host holds the Start horn.

Screen 6 — the Campfire. Host / join-by-code / open-sessions list, backend selection with honest technical labels, presence rendered as fire and seats, host-only Start. Every path in FR-SHOME-5.4, one metaphor.

7 · Multiplayer match → The Board Race

BeforeThe lobby and code-sharing already work; the FRs specify a lobby, presence/heartbeat, a turn-based board, a live event stream, host/join by code or open list, and a selectable, remembered backend (Photon / Supabase).

AfterThe turn-based board the FRs call for is rendered as an actual 2D board: a winding track of tiles over the island, one token per player (their uploaded Profile avatar, FR-PROF-5), a clear turn marker, and word-gate tiles that trigger the same gate puzzles. Presence (heartbeat) shows as tokens gently bobbing; a player who drops goes grey. The live event stream becomes the ticker ("Neo solved titrant +10"), and the emote row floats reactions above your token. The lobby keeps the room code front and centre (kids dictate it across the classroom), plus the open-sessions list for joining without a code. The network choice stays — one small toggle on the host sheet, labelled in kid terms ("Instant" / "Saved games") with the technical names beneath, remembered per device exactly as specified.

AFTER — Board Race. Lobby → board → turns → events → leave, exactly the FR-SHOME-5.4 feature set; “?” tiles reuse the gate puzzles so multiplayer and solo share one challenge system.

8 · Scheduling → the Expedition Planner

Complete in v3FR-SHOME-5.5 in full, designed instead of described. From any island's detail sheet, "🗓 Plan" opens the planner: pick a day on the week strip, set the time, add a note, and a tent is pitched on that island. Reschedule drags the tent to a new day; unschedule strikes camp. The hidden toggle becomes "private camp" — friends can't see it — and the existing "Plan with others" list stays: classmates who scheduled the same trail (and didn't hide it) appear as tents on a shared timeline, which quietly turns scheduling into a social nudge to read together.

Screen 8 — the Expedition Planner. Schedule / reschedule / unschedule / hide-toggle / note, all per FR-SHOME-5.5, with the “Plan with others” list preserved as a social reading nudge.

9 · Finishing → Trail Complete (submission stays first-class)

AfterReaching the end of the trail triggers the celebration and the paperwork, in that order on screen but as one action in code: the game result is recorded as a submission through the existing path (FR-SHOME-5.3), and the screen says so explicitly — students and tutors must both trust that playing counted. Marks stay real (“17/20”); stars are derived for the child (≥90% ★★★, ≥70% ★★, finished ★); XP and streak tick up; “Reset trail” remains available afterwards for replays per FR-SHOME-5.2, with the FR-defined semantics (clears the resume point).

AFTER — Trail Complete. Real marks first, stars derived, submission confirmed on screen; Reset trail keeps FR-SHOME-5.2 replay semantics.

Audio plan

One music identity, layered: a warm 8-bar camp theme (Base Camp / Quest Map) that gains a percussion layer inside the Trek and a brighter melodic layer during the Board Race — continuity instead of jarring track switches. SFX budget: ~24 one-shots (gate clunk, word chime ×3 tiers, star slam, seal stamp, tent pitch, fire crackle, token hop, emote pop, soft error, button boop, roll, combo tick, journal page turn). All audio behind a mute toggle and duckable for the read-aloud voice.

Reading accessibility (non-negotiable for a reading game)

A dyslexia-friendly font option (e.g. Andika or OpenDyslexic) as a settings toggle applied to the ribbon and puzzles; text size S/M/L; optional read-aloud (TTS or tutor-recorded audio when available) with word-sync highlight; reduced-motion mode that swaps particles and shakes for fades (juice degrades gracefully); colour choices that never encode meaning alone (the storm cloud also prints "overdue"). These ship in Phase 5 alongside the juice — polish and accessibility are the same pass.

2D implementation plan (Unity specifics)

Hybrid architecture, MVVM preserved. Keep the UI Toolkit shell exactly as audited. Add two Unity 2D scenes (or one scene with two modes): TrekScene and BoardRaceScene, loaded additively when the student starts an attempt and unloaded on exit — so NavigationManager keeps owning page GameObjects (GEN-1/2) and the scenes behave like just another “page”. Each scene has a thin presenter that binds to the same ViewModels the current game already uses; per the FR architecture rule, the scene renders state and forwards input, nothing more.

The Trek scene, concretely. Three parallax layers (sky, mountains, treeline) as sprites scrolled at fractional camera speed; ground as a Sprite Shape or segmented tiles; Kai as a sprite with a 3-state Animator (idle / walk / celebrate) moved by tween, not physics — deterministic movement means no input skill required, no physics edge cases on low-end devices, and trivially serialisable position for resume. The passage drives everything: a TrailBuilder converts the assignment content (passage + hidden-word challenges you already parse) into an ordered list of GateData and spaces gates along the path; the story ribbon is a UI Toolkit overlay whose scroll position is bound to Kai’s progress. Gate puzzles are your existing challenge UXML panels, restyled, shown by the overlay UIDocument. Timer, points, Pass and emotes stay in the HUD overlay bound to the current game ViewModel.

The Board Race scene. The board is data (tile list with word-gate flags) rendered as sprites along a spline; tokens are sprites textured with each player’s Profile avatar. All networking stays in the existing services — presence/heartbeat maps to token bob/grey-out, the event stream feeds the ticker verbatim, turns enable/disable the Roll button. Nothing about Photon/Supabase selection changes except its label.

Performance and reach. Target the classroom floor: sprite atlas per biome, no per-frame allocations in the ribbon binding, URP 2D with lights off by default, and everything readable at 1280×720. Offline treks must run with zero network calls (content already downloaded per FR-SHOME-5.6), queueing the submission for when connectivity returns — surfaced honestly via the offline banner (“will submit when back online”).

The complete roadmap — all phases to done

Six phases, each shippable, each with acceptance criteria tied to the FRs. This is the whole journey; nothing after Phase 5 is left "to be designed later."

Phase 0 — Foundations (≈1 week)

Prepare the ground so game scenes slot into the existing architecture without breaking it. Deliverables: additive-scene loading behind NavigationManager (a "ScenePage" wrapper so GEN-1/2 hold); the GateData/TrailBuilder pipeline that converts existing assignment content into an ordered gate list; palette/typography tokens as USS variables shared by shell and overlays; Kai's sprite sheet commissioned (idle / walk / celebrate / sit, ~24 frames). Done when: an empty Trek scene loads from the current assignment detail and returns, with back-stack and placeholder mode (GEN-4) intact.

Phase 1 — The Trek (≈3–4 weeks)

The core reading game. Deliverables: parallax + path rendering (default Netville Meadows biome); Kai movement (tweened, deterministic); story ribbon bound to progress with dimmed-read / lit-current / muted-upcoming states; the three gate puzzles restyled as overlay panels; timer, points, Pass-as-detour, emote row; checkpoint save/restore mapped to the existing per-device resume point; Trail Complete with marks, stars, submission confirmation. Done when: FR-SHOME-5.2 and 5.3 pass QA entirely inside the new scene, including leave-and-resume and offline play of a downloaded assignment.

Phase 2 — Quest Map & Base Camp (≈2–3 weeks)

The world around the game. Deliverables: island map view replacing the assignments list (islands generated from assignment data, ordered by due date); legend-chip filters, class filter, title search (FR-SHOME-5.1); island detail sheet wiring start/reset/submit/download; storm-cloud due-date treatment with printed dates; Base Camp visual replacing the dashboard (hero trailhead, signposts, quick links); empty-state spyglass deep-linking to Academies → Discover (5.8); dashboard deep link to island detail (5.7). Done when: every FR-SHOME-5.x action reachable today is reachable on the map, and the shell back-stack (FR-SHELL-6) pops map → detail → camp correctly.

Phase 3 — Campfire & Board Race (≈3–4 weeks)

Multiplayer, on the board it always wanted to be. Deliverables: Campfire host/join screen (fire-type backend toggle, remembered per device; join by code; open-campfires list); lobby with seats, presence flare/grey-out, host-only start; the Board Race scene (tile track from data, avatar tokens, turn marker, dice/advance action, "?" tiles invoking the shared gate puzzles); event-stream ticker and replayed opponent animations; leave-and-rejoin. Done when: FR-SHOME-5.4 passes QA on both backends with 2–4 players across two devices, including a mid-game disconnect.

Phase 4 — Meta & planning (≈2 weeks)

The layer that brings students back. Deliverables: Word Journal (card mint on solve, tiers, silhouettes, search, word-of-the-day on Base Camp); Reader Ranks (XP from marks/completions: Page → Scribe → Wordsmith → Storykeeper → Legend) shown on Base Camp and lobby seats; streak flame with pause-not-punish rules; the Expedition Planner in full (pitch/move/strike tent, note, private-camp toggle, Plan-with-others list — FR-SHOME-5.5); optional class leaderboard card on Tutor Home (read-only). Done when: all meta values are derivable from stored marks/submissions/attempts (no new source-of-truth), verified by recomputing from a fresh sync.

Phase 5 — Fancy pass (≈2–3 weeks)

Polish and accessibility as one pass, executed from the juice checklist, audio plan and accessibility list above. Deliverables: all "moment" animations; biome skins for the subjects you actually have (start with two: Meadows + Storybook Forest); music layers + ~24 SFX behind a mute toggle; dyslexia font / text size / read-aloud / reduced-motion settings; performance sign-off on your lowest-spec target device (steady 60 fps in the Trek, no GC spikes from the ribbon). Done when: the presentation-target mockups in this document are matched on-device, and a reduced-motion run of every screen is still fully playable.
